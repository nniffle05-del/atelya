# Finding online the EXACT 3D model of a piece of furniture seen in a photo (state: 16 November 2026)

> How to read these notes.
> **VERIFIED** = the official page (or official PDF) was opened in this session and the quoted words are verbatim. The reading tool returns quotes as fragments of at most ~125 characters: where fragments are joined by "[…]" the words in between were not shown to me.
> **UNVERIFIED** = page blocked to my tool (HTTP 403 / 404 / 429 / 503 or empty page), behind a login, seen only through a search-engine excerpt, or reported only by a secondary source. The reason is given each time.
> Method limits respected: no 3D model or dataset downloaded (only three terms-of-use / press PDFs were read), no account created, nothing uploaded, no scraping, no personal or project information in any query. Two verification passes; the second was capped by the coordinator at about 15 page reads, so several manufacturer and retailer terms remain unopened and are listed under "Gaps".
>
> **Bottom line for the owner.** "Find the exact model online" is free and lawful today for ONE source only: the Amazon Berkeley Objects set already in use (licence CC BY 4.0). Every other source read is either restricted to private / non-commercial use (IKEA, MillerKnoll, Flos, Conforama), reserved to building professionals for building projects (BIMobject), personal and non-transferable without automation or collections (pCon.catalog), or allowed only inside a delivered scene and never as a library (3D Warehouse). Retailers' 3D files (IKEA's are technically reachable) may not be reused. The route therefore works only for a few percent of real furniture at best, unless brands give written permission one by one.

## 1. Identifying the product from a photo crop, for free

### Takeaway
There is no official, free, automatable "which product is this?" web service in November 2026: Google Lens has no official API, Microsoft retired the Bing Search APIs on 11 August 2025, and the Amazon, Pinterest and IKEA visual searches are consumer apps. The only free and lawful automatable route is a LOCAL index of product images that one is allowed to hold, searched with an open image encoder (SigLIP 2 is Apache 2.0); published exact-product accuracy is modest: about 30-50 % top-1 on furniture benchmarks, 56-80 % for open encoders on an easier same-domain product benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- **Google Lens: no official API found.** The only "Google Lens APIs" on offer (SerpApi, Apify, SearchApi, ScrapingBee, OpenWebNinja) are paid third-party scrapers of Google's results; Apify's own page states that no official Google Lens API exists (search-engine paraphrase; no Google page says it in so many words: UNVERIFIED). — [Apify](https://apify.com/api/google-lens-api), [SerpApi](https://serpapi.com/google-lens-api/release-notes)
- **Google's terms forbid automated access against its robots instructions.** VERIFIED, Google Terms of Service, "Effective July 30, 2026", under "Don't abuse our services": "using automated means to access content from any of our services" […] "in violation of the machine-readable instructions on our web pages" […] "(for example, robots.txt files that disallow crawling, training, or other activities)". The scraper "Lens APIs" are therefore both paid and outside Google's terms. — [Google Terms of Service](https://policies.google.com/terms?hl=en)
- **Google Cloud Vision "Product Search" searches only YOUR OWN catalogue.** VERIFIED: it "applies machine learning to compare the product in the user's query image with the images in the retailer's product set"; supported categories: homegoods, apparel, toys, packaged goods, general. It does not identify products on the open web, so it brings nothing that a local index does not. — [Google Cloud docs](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vision/product-search/docs)
- Its price: first 1,000 images per month free, then $4.50 per 1,000 (read by the search engine on a localised copy of the official pricing page; the pricing page came back truncated to my tool: UNVERIFIED). A search-engine excerpt of another official page says the feature "is in maintenance mode"; the docs page I opened carried no such notice (conflict: UNVERIFIED). — [Google Cloud pricing](https://cloud.google.com/vision/product-search/pricing)
- **Bing Visual Search API: retired.** VERIFIED, Microsoft Lifecycle: "Bing Search APIs will be retired on August 11, 2025. Any existing instances of Bing Search APIs will be decommissioned completely, and the product will no longer be available to be used or new customer signup." Suggested replacement: "Grounding with Bing Search as part of Azure AI Agents" (a paid Azure service for text answers, not a visual search). The page does not name each API; that Visual Search was among them is reported by third parties (UNVERIFIED). — [Microsoft Lifecycle](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/bing-search-api-retirement)
- **Amazon Lens / StyleSnap**: consumer features of the Amazon app (new visual-search functions announced 3 June 2026 according to the trade press); no developer API was found (absence of evidence: UNVERIFIED). — [RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.eu/news/fashion/amazon-launches-visual-search-engine/)
- **Pinterest Lens**: no official visual-search API; the official API has no pin search, and a Pinterest staff answer on the developer forum says the terms prohibit scraping, collecting or accessing Pinterest data without prior authorisation (search-engine paraphrase, forum page not opened: UNVERIFIED). — [Pinterest developer community](https://community.pinterest.biz/t/inquiry-about-using-pinterest-images-and-obtaining-official-permission/39124)
- **Open encoder licence.** VERIFIED, Hugging Face model card of Google's SigLIP 2 (so400m, patch14, 384): "License: apache-2.0"; intended for "zero-shot image classification and image-text retrieval", with example code to obtain image embeddings. — [SigLIP 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-so400m-patch14-384)
- **Open encoders on an exact-product benchmark** (Stanford Online Products test split: 60,502 query and 60,502 gallery images, 11,316 products, 12 categories, self-retrieval). VERIFIED on a vendor-run public leaderboard, Recall@1: SigLIP2 SO400M 80.28 %; PE-Core L/14 (Meta) 80.09 %; Vertex AI multimodal (Google, paid) 76.88 %; Gemini Embedding 2 75.63 %; DINOv3 ViT-L/16 66.61 %; DINOv2 Large 56.34 %; the vendor's own commercial model 87.06 %. Caveats: the benchmark is run by a vendor; the page does not say whether models are zero-shot; query and gallery are the same kind of seller photos, which is easier than "listing photo → catalogue packshot". — [nyris benchmark](https://benchmark.nyris.io/datasets/stanford_op)
- **ABO retrieval benchmark** (Amazon Berkeley Objects, paper read in its HTML mirror). VERIFIED: 562 classes / 50,756 instances; best Recall@1 with rendered-image queries 30.0 % (NormSoftmax), 29.4 % (ProxyNCA), 28.6 % (Contrastive); with catalogue-image queries 39.7 % (R@2 52.21, R@4 64.41, R@8 71.64). Authors: "Moreover, it is worth noting that the overall performance on ABO is significantly lower than for existing common benchmarks." These are 2022 baselines. — [ABO paper (ar5iv mirror)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.06199)
- **DeepFurniture** ("24k indoor images, 170k furniture instances and 20k unique furniture identities"). VERIFIED: top-1 accuracy 52.1 %, top-5 71.0 %, top-10 76.2 % for the authors' best method; 25.2 % top-1 for a pre-trained ResNet-101. The scenes are computer renderings, not photos, so real listing photos will be harder. — [DeepFurniture paper (ar5iv mirror)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.09299)
- **ABO as a lawful local index.** VERIFIED: "This work is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Public License (CC BY 4.0)."; credit for the data goes to "Amazon.com"; 147,702 products, 398,212 catalogue images, 3D models for more than 7,900 products (7,953 in the paper). — [ABO dataset page](https://amazon-berkeley-objects.s3.amazonaws.com/index.html)

### Inferences
- A cloud visual-search service would mean sending clients' photos to a third party and paying beyond a small free tier; it does not meet "zero cost". Local matching does.
- Expected exact-match rate from a real-estate photo crop (clutter, odd angle, partial view, old photo) against catalogue packshots is probably nearer the 30-50 % of the furniture benchmarks than the 56-80 % of the easy benchmark, and this is the rate AMONG products that are in the index. No published figure for "listing photo → exact catalogue product" was found.
- Because one top answer in two or three may be wrong, an "exact" model must only be used above a confidence threshold (clear lead over the second candidate, agreement between several photos of the same piece); otherwise a wrong look-alike would be placed with false certainty.

### Gaps
- IKEA's own in-app visual search was not researched.
- Google Cloud Vision "Web Detection" (an official reverse-image feature that might name a product page) was not opened: price, terms and usefulness unknown. It would in any case send client photos to Google and cost money above a free tier.
- Pix3D and "IKEA Objects" benchmark figures were not read directly; licences of DINOv3 and PE-Core were not opened (DINOv2, Apache 2.0, is already in the project's own licence notes).
- No benchmark on real in-the-wild listing photos exists in what I found.

### Inferences
- BIMobject: a real-estate visit sold to an estate agency is not "a construction or other building project to which you are a party as a professional advisor", and clause 4.7(g) forbids incorporation in a product or service provided to a third party. Not usable without a written agreement with BIMobject (or the manufacturer).
- pCon.catalog has the most generous wording found ("reproduce, edit, forward, publish, distribute"), so a person who downloads one model by hand and shows it in a rendered or streamed visit has a defensible reading. But the right is personal and non-transferable, a local index or library is "a collection", and automation is excluded. An automated library is therefore not covered; NEEDS A LAWYER or a written yes from EasternGraphics before relying on it.
- 3D Warehouse: use inside a delivered scene is expressly accepted, model by model; a stored library or index is not. Whether automated download is allowed could not be verified, so treat it as not allowed.
- Fab and Sketchfab licences clear only the 3D author's rights; they are fine for generic furniture, and they leave the brand and design questions open for replicas of real products.
- What these catalogues hold is mainly contract, office and design furniture plus building products, not French mass-market home furniture.

### Gaps
- Binding texts not readable: 3D Warehouse General Model License, the full Fab EULA; terms of Archiproducts, Syncronia, NBS Source, CADdetails, Design Connected, 3dsky, Dimensiva. Coverage counts of home furniture per aggregator were not found.

## 4. Mass-market retailers: do public 3D / AR assets exist, and may they be reused?

### Takeaway
3D files of mass-market products do exist (IKEA serves 3D models to its product-page viewer and hobby tools download them), but the retailers' terms give no right beyond consulting the site for private use, and several name scraping explicitly. ## 6. Legal frame in France / EU, in plain terms (items marked NEEDS A LAWYER)

### Takeaway
Three separate locks. (a) Taking files from a website against its terms: breach of contract, plus the database right when a substantial part, or repeated small parts, are extracted. (b) The furniture itself: original designs are protected by copyright and French courts have condemned commercial images in which recognisable designer furniture was prominent; design rights add a second layer that since May 2025 expressly reaches digital files. (c) Brand names and logos are trademarks. The text-and-data-mining exception allows analysing lawfully accessible content, not reusing a retailer's 3D model inside a product.

### Cited Findings
- **Database right (scraping).** VERIFIED on the UK government's copy of Directive 96/9/EC (revised version 06/06/2019), art. 7(1): "Member States shall provide for a right for the maker of a database" […] "which shows that there has been qualitatively and/or quantitatively a substantial investment" […] "to prevent extraction and/or re-utilization of the whole or of a substantial part"; art. 7(5): "The repeated and systematic extraction and/or re-utilization of insubstantial parts of the contents of the database" […] "implying acts which conflict with a normal exploitation of that database or which unreasonably prejudice" […] "the legitimate interests of the maker of the database shall not be permitted." — [Directive 96/9/EC art. 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/1996/9/article/7)
- **Site terms bind even without a database right; French courts apply the database right to listing sites.** CJEU C-30/14 Ryanair v PR Aviation: a site owner may restrict use of an unprotected database by its terms; Cour de cassation, 5 October 2022, n° 21-16.307, Leboncoin v Entreparticuliers: extraction and reuse of a substantial part of the adverts sub-database condemned (law-firm summaries seen through the search engine; decisions not opened: UNVERIFIED). — [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/website-operators-can-prohibit-screen-scraping-of-unprotected-data-via-terms-and-conditions-says-eu-court-in-ryanair-case), [CMS France](https://cms.law/en/fra/news-information/arret-leboncoin-web-scraping-droit-sui-generis-sur-les-bases-de-donnees)
- **Text-and-data-mining exception.** VERIFIED on the UK government's copy of Directive (EU) 2019/790, art. 4: an exception for "reproductions and extractions of lawfully accessible works" for text and data mining; copies may be kept "for as long as is necessary for the purposes of text and data mining."; it applies only if the rightholder has not "expressly reserved" the use "in an appropriate manner" (for online content, by machine-readable means). — [Directive (EU) 2019/790 art. 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2019/790/article/4)
- **Designer furniture in commercial images (France).** Paris Court of Appeal, 13 June 2014, Fondation Le Corbusier v Getty Images, as reported by a specialist blog (judgment not opened: UNVERIFIED): infringement for the photographs where the furniture was "clearly identifiable in particular in their original aspects, central position in the foreground"; a reproduction "escapes liability provided it is incidental, fortuitous or accessory to some principal object"; damages of "€1,800 per infringing photograph". An earlier round ended with the Cour de cassation rejecting Getty's appeal on 12 June 2012. The furniture dates from the 1920s and its designer died in 1965: protection was still being enforced in 2014. — [The 1709 Blog](https://the1709blog.blogspot.com/2014/08/le-corbusier-heirs-score-win-over-getty.html)
- **Copyright in furniture (EU).** CJEU, 4 December 2025, joined cases C-580/23 (Mio) and C-795/23 (konektra), both about furniture: works of applied art are judged by the same originality test as any other work, with no higher bar; infringement turns on the recognisable taking of the creative elements (law-firm commentaries seen through the search engine, judgment not opened: UNVERIFIED). — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/nld/legal-updates/o-sole-mio-cjeu-s-tune-on-copyright-protection-of-works-of-applied-art), [DDG](https://en.ddg.fr/actualite/mio-konektra-copyright-protection-for-design-works-the-cjeu-clarifies-originality-and-infringement)
- **Design right, basic rules** (UK government's retained copy of Regulation (EC) 6/2002, i.e. the wording BEFORE the 2024 EU reform; fragments VERIFIED on that copy): a design is "the appearance of the whole or a part of a product resulting from the features of" […] "in particular, the lines, contours, colours, shape, texture and/or materials of the product itself"; the right gives "the exclusive right to use it", where use covers "the making, offering, putting on the market," "importing, exporting or using of a product in which the design is incorporated"; an unregistered design lasts "for a period of three years" and is infringed "only if the contested use results from copying the protected design."; exceptions: "(a) acts done privately and for non-commercial purposes;" "(b) acts done for experimental purposes;" "(c) acts of reproduction for the purpose of making citations or of teaching". — [Regulation (EC) 6/2002, Title II](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2002/6/title/II)
- **EU design reform** (Regulation (EU) 2024/2822), as described by a law firm (secondary source, read): "May 1, 2025, marked the entry into force of most provisions of Phase I"; Phase II "will enter into force as of July 1, 2026."; the definitions now cover "non-physical (dematerialized) products"; it is prohibited "to create, download, copy, share, or distribute any medium or software reproducing the design or model" for the purpose of making a product (art. 19(2)(d)); new exceptions: "Referential use (reproduction to identify a product or refer to it)" and "Parody, commentary, and criticism, in compliance with freedom of expression."; "Cumulative protection by design law and copyright is confirmed". EUR-Lex itself returned an empty page to my tool, so the official wording is UNVERIFIED. — [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/05/revolutionizing-eu-design-protection_key-changes-under-new-designs-and-models-regulation)
- **The French trade is alert to design copying.** VERIFIED (trade bodies' press kit, 3 February 2026): a seat sold online « semble être une contrefaçon de l’iconique Togo de Roset »; « La commercialisation de contrefaçon est une pratique largement répandue sur les plateformes chinoises, détruisant de la valeur pour les entreprises françaises investissant dans le design et la création. » — [Ameublement français / CNEF / IPEA press kit](https://www.ameublement.com/uploads/attachments/confrence-ameublement-26---dossier-de-presse-v30012026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Text and data mining does not rescue the idea.** It could at most cover computing "fingerprints" from product photos one may lawfully view, where the site has not reserved its rights; several retailers do reserve them in their terms (Habitat, Conforama and Flos name scraping). It never covers keeping a retailer's 3D model and showing it in a client's visit: that is reuse, not analysis. And the owner refuses scraping anyway.
- **Recognisable designer piece in a paid visit: NEEDS A LAWYER.** A 3D model placed on purpose, recognisable, and inspectable from all sides in a visit sold to a client is closer to the Getty case (identifiable, in the foreground) than to an object that merely happens to be in the background. For design classics (Togo, Le Corbusier, Eames and the like) the safe options are the manufacturer's own file with written permission, or a clearly generic look-alike.
- **Ordinary mass-market furniture.** The copyright risk is lower when a shape has little originality, but a registered design may exist for a given product, and an unregistered one protects any new design for three years against copying. Advertising "the exact model of brand X" increases exposure. NEEDS A LAWYER before making it a selling point.
- **Manufacturer's file under the manufacturer's terms** is the one case where the design owner has agreed to the reproduction, within the purpose it states; that purpose must cover a paid interactive visit, which none of the terms read says explicitly. Whether a packaged Unreal application handed to a client "transfers" or "redistributes" the file, and whether streaming from a rented cloud PC avoids this, NEEDS A LAWYER.
- **Brand names and logos**: keep them out of the delivered visit and of the marketing unless a lawyer confirms the wording; the 3D shape and the brand name are two different rights.

### Gaps
- Not verified on an official text in this session: the term of copyright after the designer's death; the maximum term of a registered design; the trademark "referential use" rule (the page for Regulation (EU) 2017/1001 art. 14 showed only its heading); the post-reform wording of the design regulation (arts 3, 19, 20); the French transposition of the mining exception; the French case law on the "accessory" doctrine other than through the blog cited.

## 8. Best zero-cost, lawful workflow that can be substantiated

### Takeaway
Keep the present method and add an "exact match only when sure" step on top: a local index built only from sources that allow it (ABO now; later, brand files obtained with written permission), searched locally with an open encoder; use the exact model above a confidence threshold, otherwise fall back to the closest generic model by category, size and colour. Its limit is coverage, not technique.

### Cited Findings
- ABO is the only source read whose licence expressly covers commercial reuse of both the product photos and the 3D models: "This work is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International Public License (CC BY 4.0)." with credit to "Amazon.com". — [ABO dataset page](https://amazon-berkeley-objects.s3.amazonaws.com/index.html)
- An open encoder with a permissive licence is available for the matching: SigLIP 2, "License: apache-2.0"; on the product benchmark it finds the right product first in 80.28 % of cases against 56.34 % for DINOv2 Large. — [SigLIP 2 model card](https://huggingface.co/google/siglip2-so400m-patch14-384), [nyris benchmark](https://benchmark.nyris.io/datasets/stanford_op)
- Every other source read restricts use to private or non-commercial purposes (IKEA, MillerKnoll, Flos, Conforama), to building projects (BIMobject), to personal non-transferable use without collections or automation (pCon.catalog), or to combined works without aggregation (3D Warehouse). — [IKEA.fr CGU](https://www.ikea.com/fr/fr/customer-service/terms-conditions/conditions-generales-utilisations-pub9f645241/), [BIMobject User Terms](https://business.bimobject.com/terms-of-service-eula), [pCon.catalog GTC](https://easterngraphics.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/EGR_pCon.catalog_GTC_20240529.pdf), [3D Warehouse FAQ](https://help.sketchup.com/en/3d-warehouse/3d-warehouse-terms-use-faq)

### Inferences
- **Workflow.** (1) Crop each detected piece of furniture. (2) Compute its fingerprint locally with an open encoder. (3) Look for the nearest neighbours among ABO's 398,212 catalogue images, within the same category. (4) Accept the exact ABO model only if the best candidate is clearly ahead of the second and the different photos of the same piece agree. (5) Otherwise keep today's behaviour (category, size, colour). (6) For brands seen often, write to the manufacturer for explicit permission (use of its 3D files in paid real-time visits, stored in a private library, no redistribution of the files) and add its files brand by brand. (7) Write to IKEA France as well: without a written yes, IKEA files stay out.
- **Limits.** ABO products are rare in French homes, so the visible gain on real listings will be small; the matching will be wrong often enough that the threshold must be strict; "exactly the same as in the photo" cannot be promised for most pieces by this route. Its real value is a clean mechanism that can absorb any catalogue for which permission is later obtained.
- A trial with a different encoder (SigLIP 2 in place of DINOv2) would be a new model download: it needs the owner's written agreement and its weight licence should be re-read at that time.

### Gaps
- No measurement of the real match rate on French listings; no brand has been asked yet, so the yield of the "written permission" route is unknown.
