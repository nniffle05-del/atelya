## 1. Single photo: "depth shell" from a masked metric depth map, completed on the unseen side, textured with the photo

### Takeaway
The depth shell is cheap, licence-clean and mostly already available in the tools in place (MoGe MIT exports a textured mesh with edge removal, SAM 2 Apache gives the mask, Open3D / trimesh / xatlas MIT do the rest); it reproduces the photo exactly from the photo's viewpoint, but from elsewhere it is only a front skin, and published pipelines report stretched faces, holes and smeared texture. The unseen side must be closed by symmetry, by a box/primitive, by the room geometry (floor, walls) or by a learned prior; no ready-made, permissively licensed "depth shell + symmetry completion" tool for furniture was found, so it has to be assembled.

### Inferences
- Recipe with what is in place: MoGe-2 metric points + normals → SAM 2 mask → triangulate the masked pixel grid → drop faces by edge length and by normal-to-view angle (Text2Room's two filters, MoGe's `--threshold`) → the photo itself is the texture (UV = pixel coordinates, later repacked). Seconds per object on 8 GB.
- The apartment model gives three priors the robotics papers did not have: (1) the floor plane (objects stand on it, the shell can be closed down to the floor; this also fixes the depth-extent ambiguity quoted from Gen3DSR); (2) the walls (a piece standing against a wall never shows its back in a free visit: a flat back on the wall plane is enough); (3) the vertical, which restricts a mirror plane to vertical planes (2 unknowns), exactly the "frame aligned with the supporting surface" assumption of Bohg et al.
- Mirroring suits the left/right symmetry of sofas, beds, chairs, cabinets seen 3/4 front; it creates neither the back nor the underside, and it duplicates baked shadows, highlights and any occluder left in the mask.
- "The same furniture as in the photo" is therefore true for the visible skin only; hidden faces are guesses whatever the method. A learned prior can supply a plausible hidden side that is then scaled and registered to the metric shell, the photo being re-projected on the visible faces.
- 8 GB today: the whole shell pipeline, mirror/box closure, TripoSR as a prior. A 24-48 GB cloud GPU (and Linux) unlocks TRELLIS / TRELLIS.2, Amodal3R and SAM 3D Objects, but their official mesh/texture post-processing must be replaced (nvdiffrast, Inria-derived rasterisers) by own code (marching cubes, xatlas, own projection/baking).

### Gaps
- No benchmark found that measures a symmetry- or box-completed depth shell of furniture from novel viewpoints (robotics papers measure graspability, not appearance).
- Reflect3D: no code; NeRD: checkpoint licence and training-data terms not established; ISCNet: paper unreadable here.
- TRELLIS.2 VRAM; whether TRELLIS / SAM 3D Objects can output a mesh without nvdiffrast; SAM 3D Objects access form.
- Whether MoGe-3 (FlexGEMM / Triton) installs on Windows and fits 8 GB.

## 2. Two to four photos of the same object with KNOWN cameras: classical options, sparse-view and feed-forward methods

### Takeaway
After the licence filter, the usable multi-view geometry engines are Depth Anything 3 BASE / SMALL (Apache), MapAnything "apache" (Apache, accepts known poses and intrinsics as inputs) and MoGe (MIT), fused with classical tools (Open3D, VDBFusion, OpenVDB: MIT / Apache). Almost every published few-shot Gaussian-splatting code (FSGS, DNGaussian, SparseGS, CoR-GS, Binocular3DGS, 3DGS-MCMC, Sparse2DGS) sits on the non-commercial Inria licence; the Depth Anything 3 gaussian head exists only in CC BY-NC checkpoints; VGGT has ONE commercially licensed checkpoint (gated, account and data sharing required, military use excluded) while VGGT-Omega is non-commercial; FreeSplatter excludes the EU; the MIT-labelled feed-forward splatters are scene-level view-synthesis models that neither complete hidden sides nor output meshes, and two of them inherit non-commercial initial weights.

### Inferences
- Poses known to 30-40 cm are far too coarse for direct fusion or carving of furniture (a 1-3 cm voxel grid needs centimetre-level relative poses; a carved hull is the intersection of cones, so a misplaced cone cuts real matter away). Every multi-view route must re-register the views first: (1) run one network on the 2-4 photos together to get mutually consistent depth and relative poses (DA3-BASE, or MapAnything-apache fed with the known intrinsics/poses as priors); (2) anchor that block to the apartment by fitting its floor and wall points to the planes of the plan (more robust than trusting the camera positions); (3) refine per object with ICP on the masked points; (4) fuse (TSDF 1-2 cm) and carve with masks and with free space seen by the depth maps.
- The only cleared any-view DA3 checkpoints (BASE, SMALL) are the weakest of the family in the paper's reconstruction table (HiRoom F1 25.9 for Base against 69.5 for Large and 85.1 for Giant). This is the main quality ceiling of pipeline B today; three ways around it, all to be tested: the DA3-LARGE-1.1 licence question (0.35B parameters, would plausibly fit 8 GB), MapAnything-apache in "MVS mode" with known cameras, or VGGT-1B-Commercial if the owner accepts the gated form and Meta's use policy.
- With only 2-4 silhouettes the visual hull is a loose bounding volume; it is useful as a constraint (nothing outside the cones, floor/wall clipping), not as the surface.
- Feed-forward splatters are not worth integrating for this goal: scene-level models trained on RealEstate10K / DL3DV at 256-768 px, pixel-aligned gaussians only where the inputs look, accurate poses required (DepthSplat), no mesh, partly doubtful weight ancestry.
- A self-written few-shot optimiser on gsplat is legally possible (the published regularisers must be re-implemented, their codes being Inria-licensed), but the published 3-view numbers (about 20 dB on forward-facing scenes) and the stated inability to handle unobserved regions predict a modest gain over fused depth for 2-4 views.
- 8 GB today: DA3-BASE / SMALL, MapAnything-apache, MoGe, Open3D / VDB fusion, object-scale gsplat. A 24-48 GB GPU unlocks nothing decisive in this section under the licence filter (bigger DA3 checkpoints are CC BY-NC; VGGT-Omega is non-commercial); Linux cloud machines do make VDBFusion and other Linux-first tools easier.

### Gaps

- The LICENSE file inside the gated VGGT-1B-Commercial repository; whether "sharing Hugging Face information" is acceptable to the owner.

## 3. From gaussians or point clouds to a clean textured mesh for Unreal Engine

### Takeaway
A fully permissive chain exists and is classical: points or rendered depth → TSDF / VDB fusion or Poisson → marching cubes → clean-up and decimation → UV atlas (xatlas, or Open3D's UVAtlas-based unwrap) → projection of the calibrated photos into the atlas (Open3D `project_images_to_albedo`, MVS-Texturing, or Blender baking) → glTF. gsplat (Apache) has its own 2D-gaussian rasteriser with depth and normal outputs, so splats can be meshed without Inria code; the well-known neural mesh extractors (SuGaR, 2DGS reference code, GOF, RaDe-GS, MILo, PGSR) are all non-commercial.

### Inferences

- Two interchangeable texture routes, both free of new GPL/NC code: (a) Python side: xatlas UVs + Open3D `project_images_to_albedo` (CPU, blends overlapping photos, warns about seams when exposure differs: exactly the case of listing photos); (b) Blender side: project each photo from its calibrated camera onto the mesh and bake ("Selected to Active" / emit) into one atlas with margin. Route (b) reuses the existing automatic Blender atelier.
- Occlusion-aware projection is required in both routes (a photo must not paint faces it does not see); Open3D's documentation does not describe its visibility handling, so this must be tested.
- At the input resolution (objects 200-500 px wide) a 1k atlas per object already exceeds the information in the photos; triangle budgets are small after quadric decimation.

## 4. Completing hidden parts before reconstruction: inpainting and amodal completion with commercially usable licences

### Takeaway
Commercially usable inpainting exists at every size: LaMa (Apache 2.0, small, no prompt), Stable Diffusion 1.5 / 2 / SDXL inpainting (OpenRAIL-M / OpenRAIL++-M: no non-commercial clause, use-based restrictions to pass on, licensor "claims no rights in the Output"), FLUX.1 [schnell] and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (Apache 2.0), Qwen-Image-Edit (Apache 2.0). The purpose-built tools are closed to commercial use: FLUX.1 Fill [dev] and Kontext [dev] (non-commercial model licence) and pix2gestalt (CC BY-NC 4.0, 22-28 GB). Whatever these models paint is invented, and published evidence shows that "2D completion, then 3D" is clearly worse than 3D-aware completion.

## 5. Evidence on achievable quality with 1-3 views of furniture in cluttered indoor photos

### Takeaway
The published numbers agree: methods that use only the observed pixels reach PSNR 16-24 dB with 2-6 real views and state that they cannot render what no input saw; surface reconstruction from 3 calibrated views reaches about 1-3 mm Chamfer only on the clean, precisely calibrated DTU benchmark; single-view scene pipelines reach F-scores around 55-75 % per object and are "limited by [their] weakest link"; occluded objects need a 3D prior. No benchmark matches the real case (wide-angle listing photos, furniture 200-500 px wide, partly hidden, poses known to 30-40 cm): real quality has to be measured on the company's own dwellings.

### Cited Findings
- 3 views, real forward-facing scenes (LLFF): 3D-GS 17.43 dB, FSGS 20.31 dB; FSGS "cannot generalize to occluded views that are unobserved during training" — [FSGS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00451)
- 2 / 4 / 6 views, real scenes (DL3DV), feed-forward with known poses: DepthSplat 19.31 / 23.12 / 24.19 dB — [DepthSplat paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13862)
- 3 views, Mip-NeRF360, pose-free feed-forward: AnySplat 16.20 dB — [AnySplat paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23716)
- Multi-view depth + TSDF fusion (DA3 paper, numbers as extracted from the HTML version): the Apache DA3-Base scores F1 25.9 on HiRoom (indoor) and 47.2 on ScanNet++, against 69.5 / 67.9 for DA3-Large and 85.1 / 77.0 for DA3-Giant; DTU Chamfer 2.87 mm (Base), 2.08 mm (Large), 1.85 mm (Giant); ground-truth poses lift DA3-Giant to 95.6 on HiRoom — [DA3 paper, HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10647)
- Sparse-view surface reconstruction on DTU with 3 views (search summary of the papers, not checked at source): Sparse2DGS Chamfer 1.13 against 2DGS 2.81, GOF 2.82, PGSR 2.08; FatesGS 1.37 ("little-overlap" setting); SparseSurf 1.05 — UNVERIFIED — [Sparse2DGS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20378), [FatesGS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04628), [SparseSurf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14633)
- Single photo, cluttered scene, modular pipeline (Gen3DSR, 3DV 2025; depth + entity segmentation + amodal completion + per-object generative reconstruction + scale alignment to the visible depth): full scene on 3D-FRONT Chamfer 0.099 / F-score 75.33 (InstPIFu 0.119 / 70.63; BUOL 0.294 / 37.04; Uni-3D 0.448 / 32.97); foreground objects: 3D-FRONT 0.120 / 68.82, real HOPE-Image objects 1.446 / 54.82; without the reprojection/alignment step 0.155 / 58.56; runtime on an RTX A5000: about 80 s of scene analysis plus about 90 s (DreamGaussian) or 50 s (LaRa) per object — VERIFIED (fetched) — [Gen3DSR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03421)
- Failure modes stated by Gen3DSR: "errors can propagate from one stage to the next. Therefore, the performance of the overall pipeline is limited by its weakest link."; "when the camera calibration or the scale and offset estimation fail to match the correct units, the reconstructed scene will have an erroneous structure."; "the method does not address potential physical impossibilities such as intersecting objects."; the object model "sometimes overestimates the size of the object along the z-dimension, when it cannot be observed in the input view." — [Gen3DSR paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03421)
- Occlusion: "2D amodal completion followed by 3D reconstruction" is "substantially" outperformed by a 3D-aware model — [Amodal3R abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13439); numbers in section 4.
- State of the art for cluttered natural photos is a large generative model: SAM 3D "excels in natural images, where occlusion and scene clutter are common", "with at least a 5:1 win rate in human preference tests on real-world objects and scenes" — [SAM 3D abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16624)
- Depth-map meshes seen from elsewhere: "stretched out 3D geometry", "holes that were not observed by any camera", thresholds that "may not detect all stretched-out regions" — [Text2Room, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11989)
- Photo-projected texture, lighting: textures "without embedded illumination information" are what "allows the textures to be re-lighted or re-edited within modern graphics pipelines" (Paint3D's stated motivation for "lighting-less" textures) — VERIFIED (fetched) — [Paint3D abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.13913)
- Photo-projected texture, seams: "For best results, use images captured with exposure and white balance lock to reduce the chance of seams in the output texture." — [Open3D TriangleMesh API](https://www.open3d.org/docs/latest/python_api/open3d.t.geometry.TriangleMesh.html)
- Photo-projected texture, stretching: filtering faces by viewing angle "avoids creating texture for large regions of the mesh from a comparatively small number of pixels from an image" — [Text2Room, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11989)
- Research in 2026 on exactly this setting relies on generative per-object reconstruction plus layout optimisation, not on pure depth fusion (FurnSet, arXiv 2604.20093, evaluated on 3D-Future / 3D-Front; "Seeing Through Clutter", arXiv 2602.04053; DeOcc-1-to-3, AAAI 2026) — seen through a search summary only, UNVERIFIED — [FurnSet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.20093), [Seeing Through Clutter](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04053), [DeOcc-1-to-3](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/37820)

### Inferences
- From the photo's viewpoint a photo-textured shell is faithful by construction. Away from it, three things appear in order: grazing surfaces smear (few pixels over a large area), cut edges open into holes, then the missing back shows. With one photo the convincing range is a cone around the original view; mirror / box / wall closure widens it for symmetric, box-like pieces.
- Projected photo texture carries the lighting of the shot (cast shadows, window highlights, exposure and white balance). In Unreal with Lumen that lighting is applied a second time; two photos with different exposure give seams unless colours are equalised. Intrinsic decomposition (Marigold-IID, OpenRAIL++) is the licence-clean way to attenuate it, with the errors of a diffusion model.
- Thin parts (chair and table legs, lamp arms), glass, mirrors and glossy fronts are typical failures of monocular depth at 200-500 px object width, for every pipeline; AnySplat's stated artifacts on "thin structures" and "specular highlights" point the same way.
- The knowledge of the room (metric plan, floor, walls, camera heights) removes two failure modes quoted by Gen3DSR (wrong scale/units, unknown depth extent) and is the main advantage of a self-built pipeline over generic single-image systems.

### Gaps
- No source measured how photo-projected textures on furniture degrade with viewing angle; the statements above are reasoned from the cited mechanisms.
- Sparse2DGS / FatesGS / SparseSurf numbers and the DA3 table should be re-read in the PDFs before being quoted in a report.
- Units of Gen3DSR's Chamfer distances were not extracted.

## 7. Ranking of the most promising self-built pipelines

### Takeaway
In order: (A) the single-photo depth shell closed with room priors and mirror/box, textured by the photo: build first, it is cheap, clean and runs on 8 GB; (B) multi-view fusion for pieces seen in 2-4 photos, built on the same bricks: second; (C) a permissive learned 3D prior for the hidden side, registered into the metric shell and re-textured by the photo: the upgrade that cloud GPUs unlock, with a dependency clean-up to do; (D) own splatting refinement on gsplat: optional, last. Feed-forward splatting, the DA3 gaussian head, VGGT-Omega, FreeSplatter, pix2gestalt and FLUX Fill are out for licence or fitness reasons. None of these reaches "the same furniture from every side": visible faces can be the real ones, hidden faces are always guesses.

### Cited Findings
- Every licence, number and quotation used in this ranking is cited in sections 1 to 6 (in particular: MoGe / SAM 2 / Open3D / xatlas / gsplat licences; DA3 and MapAnything checkpoint licences and the DA3-Base versus Large/Giant reconstruction scores — [DA3 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.10647); the Inria licence of few-shot splatting codes — [gaussian-splatting LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/graphdeco-inria/gaussian-splatting/HEAD/LICENSE.md); TripoSR 6 GB — [TripoSR README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR/HEAD/README.md); TRELLIS 16 GB and its nvdiffrast / Inria-derived tool chain — [TRELLIS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TRELLIS/HEAD/README.md); SAM 3D Objects 32 GB, Linux — [setup.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects/HEAD/doc/setup.md)).

### Inferences
- **A. Depth shell + room priors + mirror/box closure + photo as texture (1 photo).** Steps: SAM 2 mask → MoGe-2 metric points and normals → shell with edge/angle filters → placement in the room from the known camera, floor contact and wall planes → closure (down to the floor, flat back on the wall, vertical mirror plane found by a search that maximises overlap of mirrored points with the visible ones and with the mask, or an oriented box fitted behind the shell) → hole filling, decimation → xatlas UVs, photo projected, holes filled by mirror or LaMa → glTF via Blender. 8 GB is enough; a bigger GPU changes nothing. Estimated effort: 3-5 weeks for a first version, 2-3 more weeks of hardening on real dwellings.
- **B. Multi-view fusion (2-4 photos).** Steps: joint depth for the photos of one room (DA3-BASE, or MapAnything-apache with known intrinsics/poses) → anchoring on the plan's floor and walls → per-object ICP → TSDF/VDB fusion + mask and free-space carving → same closure and texturing as A, with best-view selection per face and exposure equalisation. 8 GB is enough. Quality ceiling today = DA3-Base. Estimated effort: 5-8 weeks on top of A.
- **C. Learned prior for the hidden side, registered to the shell.** On 8 GB: TripoSR (MIT) gives a full but coarse object from the crop; scale and pose are solved against the metric shell (ICP), the visible faces keep the shell geometry and the photo texture, the prior only fills what was never seen; LaMa / SD inpainting complete occluded strips first. On 24-48 GB Linux cloud GPUs: TRELLIS / TRELLIS.2 (MIT weights), Amodal3R (CC BY 4.0 weights, code licence to confirm) or SAM 3D Objects (SAM License, gated) give much better hidden sides, but their mesh/texture post-processing must be rewritten without nvdiffrast and Inria-derived rasterisers. Estimated effort: 3-5 weeks with TripoSR, 4-8 weeks for a cloud model including the dependency replacement. (Overlaps with the study of generative models done elsewhere.)
- **D. Own few-shot splatting on gsplat, then TSDF mesh.** Initialise gaussians from B, optimise with photo, mask, depth and normal losses (gsplat 2DGS rasteriser), optionally refine camera poses, render depth, fuse, re-texture. 8 GB is enough for one object. Gain expected: sharper edges and better registration between photos; no gain where nothing was seen; real risk of floaters with 2-4 views. Estimated effort: 4-6 weeks on top of B.
- **Not worth pursuing:** feed-forward splatting (pixelSplat, MVSplat, DepthSplat, AnySplat, NoPoSplat): no completion, no mesh, scene-level training, weight ancestry partly non-commercial; DA3 gaussian head: CC BY-NC only; FreeSplatter: EU excluded; VGGT-Omega, Fast3R, DUSt3R / MASt3R, Pi3: non-commercial or "contact the authors"; SuGaR / 2DGS reference / GOF / MILo / PGSR / RaDe-GS / GS2Mesh: non-commercial; pix2gestalt, FLUX.1 Fill / Kontext [dev], FLUX.2 klein 9B: non-commercial.
- **How far toward "the same furniture as in the photo":** from the photo's viewpoint, identical (A, B) or close (C, D); from 30-45 degrees off, good for box-like and upholstered pieces if the depth is right, fragile for thin structures; from behind or above, a plausible stand-in. All effort figures are my own estimates for one developer who already has the cameras, masks, depth and the Blender chain; they come from no source.

### Gaps
- Three owner decisions would change the ranking inside B: the DA3-LARGE-1.1 licence answer, acceptance of the gated VGGT-1B-Commercial terms, and a re-test of MapAnything-apache with known cameras as inputs.
- No measured success rate exists for any of these pipelines on listing photos; the first deliverable of A should be a measured share of furniture that passes a visual check from side views.
