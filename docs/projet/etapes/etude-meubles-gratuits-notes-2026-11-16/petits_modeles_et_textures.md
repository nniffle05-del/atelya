# Small open image-to-3D models (8 GB VRAM or less) and free texturing of shape-only meshes — state on 16 November 2026

How to read these notes. VERIFIED = I opened the LICENSE file, README, model card, paper or official page myself on 16 Nov 2026; the URL is given and the decisive words are quoted. The page reader returns quotes in pieces of at most ~125 characters, so long sentences are assembled from consecutive pieces of the same sentence. UNVERIFIED = seen only in a search summary, a forum, a vendor or aggregator page, or known from background and not re-opened in this run. Nothing was downloaded, no account was created, nothing was uploaded.
Scope change recorded (coordinator, 16 Nov 2026): at launch everything runs on RENTED 24-48 GB cloud GPUs; "free" = no per-object fee and no subscription. The 8 GB RTX 2070 (Windows 11, 16 GB RAM) matters for (1) trials today and (2) a possible fast fallback. The notes are therefore decision-oriented and short on models that cannot serve either role.

## 1. For each small model: licence of code / weights / mandatory dependencies, VRAM, time, Windows, output

### Takeaway
As published, only four families fit an 8 GB card: TripoSR (MIT, ~6 GB), Stable Fast 3D (Stability Community License, ~6 GB), SPAR3D in low-VRAM mode (same licence, ~7 GB) and TripoSG / PartCrafter (MIT, "at least 8GB", shape only); Direct3D-S2, LGM, TRELLIS, TRELLIS.2, Pixal3D and SAM 3D Objects need 10 to 32 GB as published. The licence traps are in the helpers, not in the model licences: TripoSG ships with three non-compliant helpers (BRIA RMBG-1.4 non-commercial, `diso` CC BY-NC 4.0, pymeshlab GPL-3.0), all three avoidable; nvdiffrast is STILL non-commercial and sits in CRM, LGM, MV-Adapter, TRELLIS and TRELLIS.2.

### Inferences
- On the RTX 2070 as it is: TripoSR and SF3D are comfortable (~6 GB); SPAR3D needs its low-VRAM switch (~7 GB); TripoSG and PartCrafter are at the limit (official "at least 8GB", one forum report of 7.4 GB) and need the screen and other programs to leave the card almost empty.
- TripoSG can be made licence-clean with three small changes, none of which touches the model: (1) give it an already cut-out image from SAM 2 instead of loading RMBG-1.4; (2) delete the top-level `diso` import and use the scikit-image extraction path that the code already contains (this also removes a compiled CUDA package, which helps on Windows); (3) reduce faces with another tool than pymeshlab. Until those changes are made, TripoSG "as downloaded" does not satisfy the rule "commercial use allowed for mandatory dependencies". PartCrafter very probably inherits the same three helpers (only RMBG-1.4 was checked).
- TripoSG's pins (`numpy==1.22.3`, diffusers, transformers, peft) do not match the PC's existing Python 3.13 environment: either a separate environment (an installation, to be agreed) or a local rewrite as done for the other models of the project.
- SF3D and SPAR3D need a C++ build on Windows (Visual Studio 2022) and a gated Hugging Face download (accepting conditions with an account): two steps that need the owner's explicit agreement.
- The 8 GB forks of TRELLIS / TRELLIS.2 are usable for EVALUATION on the Windows PC (the nvdiffrast licence allows "research or evaluation purposes"), not inside the commercial pipeline unless nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec are replaced; Hi3DGen shows the replacement has been done for the shape part of TRELLIS.
- On rented 24 GB cards, SAM 3D Objects is borderline (official 32 GB; 24 GB only with workarounds); 48 GB is the safe size.

### Gaps
- No official inference-VRAM figure for Hi3DGen, CraftsMan3D, CRM, Unique3D, TriplaneGaussian, TripoSplat.
- No official time per object on an RTX 2070-class card for any model (only H100 / A100 figures and one forum figure for TripoSG on a 3060 Ti).
- Not opened in this run (licences believed permissive but UNVERIFIED): rembg and its default model, open_clip, gpytoolbox, pynanoinstantmeshes, AlphaCLIP, transparent-background, torchmcubes, moderngl, spconv, xformers, StableNormal / BiRefNet weights used by Hi3DGen, the Inria-derived `diff-gaussian-rasterization` (known as non-commercial) and Wonder3D (known as AGPL) behind LGM / TriplaneGaussian / Unique3D.
- Pixal3D LICENSE / NOTICE files returned 404 at the guessed paths: the list of its third-party components was not read (it sits on TRELLIS.2, hence probably nvdiffrast / nvdiffrec).
- Default polygon counts are not stated on the pages read.

## 2. Quality evidence on furniture and household objects; position versus TRELLIS, TRELLIS.2 and SAM 3D Objects

### Takeaway

## 4. Free texturing of shape-only meshes (TripoSG, PartCrafter, Hi3DGen…), especially simple projection of the original photo with the known camera

### Takeaway
No generative texturing tool read is clean for commercial use out of the box: MV-Adapter (Apache 2.0) installs nvdiffrast (non-commercial), pymeshlab (GPL) and CV-CUDA and wants 10-16 GB; TRELLIS.2's texturing mode needs nvdiffrast / nvdiffrec and 24 GB; MaterialMVP shows no licence and needs Tencent's Hunyuan3D-Paint weights; TEXTure and SyncMVD are text-driven. Projecting the ORIGINAL photo with the known camera is possible today with permissive pieces only (xatlas MIT for the UVs, Open3D's `project_images_to_albedo` on the CPU) and gives the true colours and patterns of the visible side — but nothing on hidden sides, the room's lighting baked in, and it only works if the mesh is first aligned to the photo.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED Open3D ready-made projection: `project_images_to_albedo(images, intrinsic_matrices, extrinsic_matrices, tex_size=1024, update_material=True)`: "Create an albedo for the triangle mesh using calibrated images."; "The triangle mesh must have texture coordinates ("texture_uvs" triangle attribute)."; "This works by back projecting the images onto the texture surface."; "Overlapping images are blended together in the resulting albedo."; "For best results, use images captured with exposure and white balance lock to reduce the chance of seams in the output texture."; "This function is only supported on the CPU."; inputs are (3,3) pinhole intrinsics and (4,4) extrinsics per image. The documentation says nothing about areas seen by no image, nor about colour correction — [Open3D TriangleMesh docs](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/python_api/open3d.t.geometry.TriangleMesh.html)
- VERIFIED Open3D UV unwrapping: `compute_uvatlas` is based on "Zhou et al, "Iso-charts: Stretch-driven Mesh Parameterization using Spectral Analysis""; "Input meshes must be manifold for this method to work."; CPU only; `bake_vertex_attr_textures` / `bake_triangle_attr_textures` bake attributes into a texture — [Open3D TriangleMesh docs](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/python_api/open3d.t.geometry.TriangleMesh.html)
- VERIFIED xatlas: "MIT license"; "a small C++11 library with no external dependencies" generating UVs for baking — [repo](https://github.com/jpcy/xatlas); TripoSR already bakes its own texture with `xatlas==0.0.9` + `moderngl==5.10.0` — [TripoSR requirements](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSR/main/requirements.txt)
- VERIFIED why the photo's light is a known issue: Stability's cards for SF3D and SPAR3D describe delighting (taking the lighting of the input image out of the texture) as part of what the models output, next to roughness and metallic values (wording summarised by the page reader, not a verbatim quote) — [SF3D card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-fast-3d), [SPAR3D card](https://huggingface.co/stabilityai/stable-point-aware-3d)
- VERIFIED why alignment is the hard part: with a shape generated in its own pose and then placed by a pose estimator, the object lands much worse than with a model that outputs the pose itself (Aria Digital Twin, ADD-S@0.1: Trellis + Megapose 0.1971, Hunyuan3D-2.0 + FoundationPose 0.5992, SAM 3D joint 0.7673) — [arXiv 2511.16624](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16624); Pixal3D generates "in a pixel-aligned way, consistent with the input view" and its authors warn of "imperfect segmentation boundaries" being back-projected into small artefacts — [arXiv 2605.10922](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10922)
- VERIFIED why a texture matters: "Textured models achieve a 144.1 ELO advantage over untextured geometry (1241.1 vs. 1097.1)." — [arXiv 2506.18787](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18787)
- VERIFIED MV-Adapter: "Apache-2.0 license"; image-conditioned texturing of an existing mesh (`python -m scripts.texture_i2tex --image … --mesh …`); SDXL by default, `--variant sd21` suggested when "(VRAM<10G)"; image-to-multiview "requires about 14G GPU memory"; with geometry, SDXL "If your VRAM is enough (>16G)", SD2.1 if "smaller than 10G"; needs RealESRGAN and LaMa checkpoints and CV-CUDA; no licence stated for its own weights on the README — [README](https://github.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter). Its requirements contain `git+https://github.com/NVlabs/nvdiffrast.git`, `pymeshlab`, `cvcuda_cu12`, `open3d`, `spandrel==0.4.1` — [requirements.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/huanngzh/MV-Adapter/main/requirements.txt)
- VERIFIED TRELLIS.2: `example_texturing.py` generates PBR textures for a given shape; MIT; nvdiffrast and nvdiffrec dependencies; "at least 24GB" — [README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS.2)
- VERIFIED Paint3D: "This code is distributed under an Apache 2.0 LICENSE."; kaolin 0.13.0 + PyTorch3D; UV-position ControlNet `GeorgeQi/Paint3d_UVPos_Control`; image condition through IP-Adapter; "high-resolution, lighting-less, and diverse 2K UV texture maps"; no VRAM figure — [README](https://github.com/OpenTexture/Paint3D)
- VERIFIED SyncMVD: "The program licensed under MIT License."; PyTorch3D, Stable Diffusion v1.5, ControlNet depth / normalbae; text prompt only; optional XAtlas — [README](https://github.com/LIU-Yuxin/SyncMVD)
- VERIFIED TEXTure: MIT; kaolin 0.11.0; `stabilityai/stable-diffusion-2-depth`; text-driven — [README](https://github.com/TEXTurePaper/TEXTurePaper)
- VERIFIED MaterialMVP: no licence label, no licence text and no LICENSE file visible on the repository page; needs `custom_rasterizer`, `DifferentiableRenderer`, Real-ESRGAN and the "Hunyuan3D-Paint-v2-1" weights — [repo](https://github.com/ZebinHe/MaterialMVP)

### Inferences
- A projection recipe with permissive pieces only: (1) shape from the 8 GB model (canonical pose, unknown size); (2) align it to the photo — the object's metric depth from MoGe-2 and its SAM 2 mask give its size, position and silhouette in the known camera, so the pose can be searched by matching silhouette and depth (this step does not exist ready-made and is where the work is); (3) UVs with xatlas (or `compute_uvatlas` if the mesh is manifold); (4) `project_images_to_albedo` with the photo(s) in which the object appears, each photo first masked with the SAM 2 mask so that the wall or another piece of furniture is not painted on the object; (5) fill what no photo sees.
- Expected quality of plain projection (no measurement was found; this is reasoning from the sources above): the side facing the camera carries the real fabric, colour and pattern — exactly "the same as in the photo" — at the resolution of the crop (a 300 px-wide sofa gives about 300 texels across, soft when the film camera comes close); surfaces seen at a grazing angle are stretched; hidden sides, the back and anything behind an occluder stay empty and must be filled (mirror the visible side for symmetric furniture, or the dominant colour); shadows and highlights of the real room are baked in, which is right for the same room in similar light and wrong when Unreal relights it; any alignment error paints background along the silhouette (shrink the mask by a few pixels).
- With several photos of the same piece, Open3D blends them; since real-estate photos have different exposure and white balance (the documentation asks for them to be locked), choose one main photo per face rather than blending, or equalise colours first.
- Photo projection avoids the weakness shown in the arena: a model's own generated texture is where the 6 GB models lose to TRELLIS; a real photo on a good shape has no invented texture on the visible side.
- On the rented 24-48 GB machines the generative texturers fit in memory; their blocker becomes the licence of the rasteriser (nvdiffrast) inside MV-Adapter and TRELLIS.2, which would have to be replaced by a permissive renderer before commercial use.
- For the 6 GB models no extra texturing is needed: SF3D / SPAR3D output their own UV texture (delit, with roughness / metallic), TripoSR bakes one with xatlas.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of the visual quality of single-photo projection onto a generated shape was found.
- The Blender manual page for the UV Project modifier was opened but its body was not returned; Blender's projection and baking tools (a separate GPL tool) are therefore UNVERIFIED in this run.
- Open3D's own licence (known as MIT), PyTorch3D's (BSD), CV-CUDA's (Apache 2.0, Linux) and the licences of the diffusion weights behind MV-Adapter, Paint3D, SyncMVD and TEXTure were not opened in this run.
- Hunyuan3D-Paint was not re-opened (already excluded by the fixed rule on Tencent licences).

## 5. Partly hidden objects and several photos of the same object

### Takeaway
Every small model takes ONE cut-out image and simply invents the missing part, with no notion of what hid it; only SAM 3D Objects is designed and measured for occluded objects in real photos, and only TRELLIS (tuning-free) and Pixal3D (several views with known cameras, Sep 2026) accept several photos — all three beyond 8 GB as published. The published recipe for hidden parts is to complete the object in 2D first, then generate.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED SAM 3D Objects: "SAM 3D Objects can convert masked objects in an image, into 3D models with pose, shape, texture, and layout."; "designed to be robust in challenging natural images, handling small objects and occlusions, unusual poses, and difficult situations" — [README](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects); "SAM 3D excels in natural images, where occlusion and scene clutter are common." — [arXiv 2511.16624](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16624)
- VERIFIED Pixal3D scene recipe: segment objects (SAM 3), complete the hidden regions with a 2D image-editing model (Qwen-image-edit), generate each object with Pixal3D, then use MoGe's global point map to solve each object's scale and depth by least squares; stated limits: 2D inpainting "may introduce errors in complex occlusions", multi-view needs "known, reasonably accurate camera poses" — [arXiv 2605.10922](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10922)
- VERIFIED Pixal3D with several views: features of each view are back-projected and averaged per voxel, trained with 2 to 6 views; `inference_mv.py` "conditions the cascade on all of them at once" from a folder of views plus `transforms.json` — [arXiv 2605.10922](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10922), [README](https://github.com/TencentARC/Pixal3D)
- VERIFIED TRELLIS multi-image conditioning: "based on tuning-free algorithm without training a specialized model"; "may not give the best results for all input images" — [README](https://github.com/microsoft/TRELLIS)
- VERIFIED SPAR3D: the visible surface follows the image, the unseen surface is "mainly based on the sampled point cloud", and "This point cloud can even be edited easily in external tools or our included gradio demo." — [arXiv 2501.04689](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04689), [README](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-point-aware-3d)
- VERIFIED LGM and CRM consume the fixed synthetic views made by their own multi-view generator (ImageDream / MVDream; six generated views for CRM), not free real photos — [LGM README](https://github.com/3DTopia/LGM), [CRM README](https://github.com/thu-ml/CRM)
- VERIFIED PartCrafter has a scene checkpoint trained on 3D-Front (furnished rooms) that outputs several part meshes from one image — [README](https://github.com/wgsxm/PartCrafter)

### Inferences
- For the small models, a sofa half hidden by a coffee table must be completed in 2D before generation, otherwise the model reproduces the cut as geometry; the pieces already available (SAM 2 mask, MoGe-2 metric depth, known camera) are the same pieces Pixal3D's authors use around their generator.
- With several photos of the same piece and a single-image model: take the least hidden, most frontal, largest crop for the shape; use the other photos only as extra colour sources for the projection.
- SPAR3D's editable point cloud is the only handle a small model offers on the hidden side; feeding it a partial cloud built from MoGe-2 depth is an idea, not a documented use.

### Gaps
- No small (8 GB or less) model with real multi-photo input was found.
- Licences and VRAM of 2D completion models (for example Qwen-image-edit) were not checked in this run.
- No measurement of how SF3D / SPAR3D / TripoSG degrade with the fraction of the object hidden.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED shape quality of the pick: on real images (SA-3DAO) TripoSG F1@0.01 0.1533 / Chamfer 0.0844 versus Trellis 0.1475 / 0.0902 and SAM 3D 0.2344 / 0.0400 — [arXiv 2511.16624](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16624)
- VERIFIED human preference of the baseline: SF3D 1190, SPAR3D 1144, TripoSR 1089 versus TRELLIS 1306 (isolated generated images) — [arXiv 2506.18787](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18787)
- VERIFIED fit on 8 GB: TripoSG "CUDA-enabled GPU with at least 8GB VRAM" — [README](https://github.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSG); SF3D "about 6GB VRAM" — [README](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-fast-3d); SPAR3D "roughly 7GB" in low-VRAM mode — [README](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-point-aware-3d)
- VERIFIED licences of the pick: TripoSG MIT (code) and "License: mit" (weights), with three helpers to remove (RMBG-1.4 non-commercial, `diso` CC BY-NC 4.0, pymeshlab GPL) — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VAST-AI-Research/TripoSG/main/LICENSE), [HF card](https://huggingface.co/VAST-AI/TripoSG), [RMBG-1.4](https://huggingface.co/briaai/RMBG-1.4), [diso LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SarahWeiii/diso/main/LICENSE), [PyMeshLab](https://github.com/cnr-isti-vclab/PyMeshLab); SF3D under the Stability Community License (free under USD 1M of revenue, registration) — [LICENSE.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Stability-AI/stable-fast-3d/main/LICENSE.md)
- VERIFIED projection tool: Open3D `project_images_to_albedo`, "Create an albedo for the triangle mesh using calibrated images." — [Open3D docs](https://www.open3d.org/docs/release/python_api/open3d.t.geometry.TriangleMesh.html); UVs by xatlas (MIT) — [repo](https://github.com/jpcy/xatlas)
- VERIFIED preview of the production class on the same PC: fork authors state TRELLIS fits "8GB GPU cards, if they support half-precision" and TRELLIS.2 fits "8GB gpus, even with 1024³ voxels", both with one-click Windows installers, both still tied to nvdiffrast — [TRELLIS fork](https://github.com/IgorAherne/trellis-stable-projectorz), [TRELLIS.2 fork](https://github.com/IgorAherne/TRELLIS.2-stableprojectorz)

### Inferences
- Why this pick: (1) it is the only 8 GB route whose shape is shown to be at TRELLIS level on real photos; (2) it is MIT end to end once three helpers are removed; (3) the projection of the true photo is what delivers "the same as in the photo", and that step (alignment + projection + filling hidden sides) is the same whatever shape model is used later on the rented GPUs, so the work is not lost; (4) SF3D costs almost nothing to run beside it and answers the question "is a sub-second textured model good enough as fallback?".
- Order of trial on the PC: SF3D or TripoSR first if one wants a textured object within minutes of installation (TripoSR = MIT everywhere, weakest quality; SF3D = better, Stability licence, Visual Studio build, gated download); TripoSG + projection as the real candidate; optionally the TRELLIS.2 8 GB fork strictly as an evaluation preview of what the rented GPUs will give.
- Uncertainty, stated plainly: no benchmark on furniture or on real-estate photos exists for any of these; TripoSG at 8 GB is at the limit of the card and its 75 s per object is a forum figure on a faster card; crops of 200-500 px are below the models' 512 px input and wide-angle distortion is untested; hidden parts will be invented unless completed in 2D first; the alignment step needed for projection is unbuilt and is the main technical risk; SF3D's Windows build is "experimental".
- For production on 24-48 GB: the evidence read points to SAM 3D Objects (occlusion, pose relative to the camera, 5:1 human preference; SAM License, Linux, 32 GB official / 24 GB with workarounds) and Pixal3D (MIT, pixel-aligned, several views with known cameras) as the two to measure; the small models stay as the fallback when speed matters more than fidelity.

### Gaps
- Everything in this pick is unmeasured on the project's own photos; a side-by-side on the existing apartments is the only way to settle it.
- Any installation, download of weights, gated access or run of third-party installers needs the owner's written agreement first; none was done here.
