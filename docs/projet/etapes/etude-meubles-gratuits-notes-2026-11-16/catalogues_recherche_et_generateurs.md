# Free 3D furniture catalogues, retrieval by image, adaptation and procedural generators (state at 16 November 2026)

Reading conventions used in every section below:

- **VERIFIED** = I opened the licence / terms / dataset page myself on 16 Nov 2026; the words in quotation marks are verbatim from that page (the fetch tool returns long passages as several short quotes).
- **UNVERIFIED** = taken from a search-engine summary, a third-party page, or prior knowledge; the primary page was not opened (the reason is given when it was blocked).
- Licence rules applied for the verdicts: commercial use + inclusion in a delivered Unreal application must be allowed; no NC / research-only; no AGPL; no EU exclusion; CC BY is fine (credits); GPL / LGPL / CC BY-SA / other copyleft CONTENT must not be mixed into the Unreal product, but GPL TOOLS can be used and their output is ours; zero cost (no paid asset, no subscription).
- Scope note: the exact manufacturer's / retailer's model (IKEA and others, BIM portals) is covered by another researcher; only the facts already verified here are kept, briefly.
- Hardware is not treated as a limit (production is planned on rented cloud machines); licence and per-object cost are.
- No dataset, model or add-on was downloaded; no account was created. One local file was read: the project's own saved copy of the Unreal Engine EULA (searched for the "UE-Only" clause).

## 1. Catalogues and datasets: which free 3D furniture sources can legally go into a commercial product sold in the EU, and how big / how good are they?

### Takeaway
Beyond ABO, the only LARGE free pool with a commercial-friendly licence is Objaverse 1.0 (about 88 % CC BY 4.0 + 0.4 % CC0; about 19,000 objects tagged "furniture", of very uneven quality), i.e. the Sketchfab CC BY / CC0 models; everything else that is clean and realistic is small (Poly Haven: 85 furniture models; NVIDIA packs: about 500 to 700 residential / furniture assets "free to use in your work"; Megascans: free again since November 2026 but not a furniture library) or carries a condition or a trap (Blendkit Royalty Free: allowed in a sold application only if the assets are not easily extractable; SimReady Central: licence per asset, non-commercial ones included; 3D Warehouse: "impermissible aggregation"; Digital Twin Catalog: CC BY-SA + no incorporation into a product for sale; IKEA: non-commercial or unlicensed).

### Cited Findings

#### Objaverse 1.0 (Allen Institute; objects collected from Sketchfab)
- VERIFIED size and licence mix: "Objaverse is a Massive Dataset with 800K+ Annotated 3D Objects." / "The use of the dataset as a whole is licensed under the ODC-By v1.0 license." / "Individual objects in Objaverse are all licensed as creative commons distributable objects, and may be under the following licenses:" / "CC-BY 4.0 - 721K objects" / "CC-BY-NC 4.0 - 25K objects" / "CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0 - 52K objects" / "CC-BY-SA 4.0 - 16K objects" / "CC0 1.0 - 3.5K objects" / "The metadata will provide the license for each object." — [Hugging Face dataset card allenai/objaverse](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/objaverse)
- VERIFIED how to filter by licence from the metadata alone: `objaverse.load_annotations()` "returns a map of each specified UID to its corresponding annotations."; each annotation has a `license` field (example: `'license': 'by'`); the documentation's own filtering example keeps CC BY objects with `annotation["license"] == "by"`; other fields include tags, categories, faceCount, vertexCount, name, description, thumbnails, archives; `objaverse.load_lvis_annotations()` "maps from the LVIS category (key) to a list of object UIDs within that category (value)." — [objaverse.allenai.org documentation](https://objaverse.allenai.org/docs/objaverse-1.0/)
- VERIFIED number of furniture objects (manual category annotation of an Objaverse subset by the GObjaverse / RichDreamer team): Human-Shape 41,557; Animals 28,882; Daily-Used 220,222; **Furnitures 19,284**; Buildings&&Outdoor 116,545; Transportations 20,075; Plants 7,195; Food 5,314; Electronics 13,252; **Poor-quality 107,001** (sum of the ten classes = 579,327). The page states no licence for GObjaverse itself. — [GObjaverse README, modelscope/richdreamer](https://github.com/modelscope/richdreamer/blob/main/dataset/gobjaverse/README.md)
- VERIFIED quality problem: the Objaverse++ authors say Objaverse's "utility is limited by the predominance of low-quality models"; they "manually annotate 10,000 3D objects with detailed attributes", train a classifier for the rest and "release our enhanced dataset of approximately 500,000 curated 3D models" (out of 800K+). — [Objaverse++ (arXiv 2504.07334)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07334)
- UNVERIFIED (search summary of the TexVerse paper, Aug 2025): Objaverse "often caps textures at 1024 even when originals are 4K or 8K"; TexVerse re-collected 858,669 Sketchfab models with textures of at least 1024 px (158,518 with PBR materials, .glb), keeping "only those under distributable Creative Commons licenses" and excluding "NoAI"-tagged models, out of "about 1.6 million freely downloadable Sketchfab models". The licence of the TexVerse release itself was not checked. — [TexVerse (arXiv 2508.10868)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.10868)

### Inferences
- Objaverse 1.0 usable share: 721K CC BY + 3.5K CC0 out of the 817.5K listed = about 88.6 % usable; 9.4 % non-commercial (forbidden); 2.0 % CC BY-SA (not to be mixed into the Unreal product). If the furniture class follows the same mix, about 17,000 of the 19,284 "Furnitures" objects are CC BY / CC0; only about 579K of the 800K+ objects were categorised, so the total could be higher (up to roughly 24,000 if the rest had the same furniture share). This is an estimate, not a published count: the exact figure needs a join of the `license` field with a furniture tag, which can be done on the metadata alone, before downloading any mesh.
- "19,000 furniture objects" overstates what is usable: nearly a fifth of the categorised objects are "Poor-quality", Objaverse++ kept about 500K of 800K, and Objaverse furniture mixes scans, game props, stylised models and a minority of clean arch-viz models with arbitrary scale, orientation and texture resolution. A quality filter is mandatory; the realistic, clean subset is probably a few thousand pieces (order of magnitude, not measured).
- A Creative Commons label on Sketchfab / Objaverse only binds the uploader: it does not prove the uploader owned the design (ripped game assets, branded furniture). This residual third-party risk is the main legal weakness of the Objaverse / Sketchfab route compared with ABO, Poly Haven or NVIDIA's own packs, where the publisher is the rights holder.
- Objaverse-XL adds little for furniture: the per-object `license` column has 42 values including missing ones and software licences such as GPL, Polycam is non-commercial, and (UNVERIFIED, prior knowledge) the extra objects come mostly from GitHub repositories and 3D-printing files without textures. Objaverse 1.0 / Sketchfab is the relevant part.
- Sketchfab stays a live source after the KitBash takeover (downloads continue), but its API is designed for a logged-in END USER picking models, with credit to the author and to Sketchfab; a server-side bulk harvest "without end-user authentication" needs a contact with Sketchfab. Using the Objaverse copy of the same CC BY models avoids the API question but not the attribution duty.
- Blendkit is the largest clean, realistic free pool for arch-viz (over 22,000 free models of all kinds). Its Royalty Free licence allows a sold interactive application if assets "can't be extracted by the users in an easy way"; a packaged (cooked) Unreal build meets the ordinary reading of that condition, but ABO, Poly Haven and CC BY sources have no such condition, and automated bulk use of the library as a back-end catalogue is not addressed by the pages read (an account is required, which only the owner can decide to create).
- Megascans becoming free again (Nov 2026) matters for surfaces, plants and props, not for furniture: it is a scan library of nature, buildings and props under the Fab Standard License.
- Epic "UE-Only" content: the current Unreal EULA no longer carries the clause (the term is absent from the copy saved on 10/11/2026) and sends marketplace content to its own licence, which could not be opened. The older wording (use only in a product that requires the engine) is compatible with a shipped Unreal visit; whether an intermediate pass through Blender is allowed is NOT confirmed. Since Epic's free content is not a furniture catalogue, this open point has little weight for the furniture question.
- 3D Warehouse cannot be a catalogue for an automated product: building a library of its models for use by our software is the "impermissible aggregation" its FAQ forbids, even though a single designer may deliver a "Combined Work".

## 2. Retrieval by image: how to find, for free, the catalogue model closest to a photo crop?

### Takeaway
The free recipe that the literature actually uses is "category filter by text (CLIP or the detector's label), then DINOv2 similarity between the masked photo crop and a few rendered views of each catalogue model"; it finds the right model about three times out of four when the catalogue is tiny and contains the object, falls to about 36-42 % top-1 once a thousand distractors are added or the objects look alike, and returns only a look-alike when the object is absent; the learned 3D encoders (OpenShape, Uni3D, ULIP-2, Duoduo CLIP) are free to download but none comes with a clear commercial licence on its weights.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED direct measurement of "CLIP filter, then DINOv2 on rendered views" (OSCAR, Jan 2026): each CAD model is rendered from "K=8 viewpoints"; the query object is segmented (GroundedSAM) on a gray background; stage 1 keeps candidates by CLIP similarity to captions of the renders, stage 2 picks the best query-to-view DINOv2 similarity. Top-1 / top-10 scores: YCB-V (database of 21 household objects): DINOv2 + mask 77.38 / 75.20, DINOv2 + box 70.98, CLIP + mask 69.53 / 92.61; Housecat6D (194 objects, 10 categories): DINOv2 + mask 41.81 / 39.21, CLIP + box best at top-10 with 59.91; YCB-V + 1,030 GSO models as distractors: DINOv2 + mask 36.41 / 40.37, CLIP + mask 41.31 / 44.73. Stated weaknesses: "when objects share similar textures, image embeddings may match entirely different categories"; "additional retrieved models tend to introduce more noise than useful matches". No code or licence statement on the page. — [OSCAR (arXiv 2601.07333)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07333v1)
- VERIFIED "DINOv2 on rendered snapshots" inside a full real-to-sim pipeline (ACDC, Stanford, 2024): a CLIP text match keeps the top categories, then "DINOv2 feature distances are computed between the masked object crop and each asset's representative snapshot", then again over each candidate's orientation snapshots; library = BEHAVIOR-1K, "over 10,000 object assets". In the sim-to-sim test (inputs rendered from the same library) category AND exact model were recovered for all objects of four scenes (6/6, 8/8, 10/10, 15/15), with bounding-box IoU 0.59 to 0.74 and centre errors of about 4 to 16 cm. Real-to-sim results are qualitative only. Stated limits: "ACDC is bounded by the diversity of its underlying asset dataset"; BEHAVIOR-1K is "insufficient to densely capture the real-world distribution of objects." — [ACDC (arXiv 2410.07408)](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07408v2)
- VERIFIED retrieval from a single indoor photo with open catalogues (Diorama, ICCV 2025): retrieval uses Duoduo CLIP in two steps (a text query "a photo of CLASS" narrows the category, then the image crop re-ranks), shapes from ShapeNet, Objaverse and HSSD; shape error of the retrieved model (Chamfer distance ×10³, household objects, in-catalogue / out-of-distribution): Duoduo CLIP text+image 5.5 / 9.9; CLIP 8.5 / 11.2; OpenShape 8.6 / 25.3; text only 9.4 / 12.4; image only 6.4 / 12.0. "applications requiring precise reconstructions may be hindered by the lack of exact geometry and texture matches." — [Diorama (arXiv 2411.19492)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19492v2)
- VERIFIED supervised single-view retrieval (Wu, Ritchie, Savva, Chang, 2024): on Pix3D, CMIC reaches top-1 74.9 % / top-5 89.2 % for SEEN objects (easy set) but 0.3 % / 7.6 % for UNSEEN objects; after pre-training on a synthetic occlusion dataset (MOOS) the unseen-object top-1 becomes 34.3 % (easy) and 24.2 % (hard, occluded), top-5 62.8 % and 62.4 %; Scan2CAD top-1 27.7 %; MOOS unseen objects 58.9 % without occlusion, 49.6 % with. Databases are small (Pix3D 395 shapes, Scan2CAD 3,049, MOOS 6,209). "generalization to unseen categories of objects is an open question for future work". — [arXiv 2401.00405](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00405v1)
- VERIFIED OpenShape: repository "Apache-2.0 license"; checkpoints on Hugging Face; "Training data consists of `Objaverse/000-xxx.tar.gz`, `ShapeNet.tar.gz`, `3D-FUTURE.tar.gz`, and `ABO.tar.gz`."; zero-shot top-1 (top-5) 46.8 (77.0) on Objaverse-LVIS and 84.4 (98.0) on ModelNet40; trained on "a single A100 GPU"; demo with "3D shape retrieval (from text, image, and 3D point cloud)"; the training-data repository contains per-shape image and text features. The Hugging Face model page shows "No model card" and no licence tag. — [github.com/Colin97/OpenShape_code](https://github.com/Colin97/OpenShape_code), [huggingface.co/OpenShape/openshape-pointbert-vitg14-rgb](https://huggingface.co/OpenShape/openshape-pointbert-vitg14-rgb)
- VERIFIED Uni3D: repository listed as "MIT license"; weights at huggingface.co/BAAI/Uni3D, which "range from 6M to **1B** parameters"; zero-shot top-1 (top-5): Uni3D-g 55.3 (82.9) on Objaverse-LVIS, 88.2 (99.3) on ModelNet40, 65.3 (92.7) on ScanObjectNN; Uni3D-B 51.7 (80.8) on Objaverse-LVIS; pre-trained "on ensemble datasets". The Hugging Face page shows "No model card" and no licence tag. — [github.com/baaivision/Uni3D](https://github.com/baaivision/Uni3D), [huggingface.co/BAAI/Uni3D](https://huggingface.co/BAAI/Uni3D)
- VERIFIED ULIP / ULIP-2 (Salesforce): code BSD-3-Clause; "The released "ULIP - Objaverse Triplets" is under" ODC-BY 1.0, "consistent with Objaverse's license."; ShapeNet triplets follow ShapeNet's terms of use; no separate licence is given for the weights; ULIP2-PointBERT reaches 50.6 / 79.1 (Objaverse top-1 / top-5) and 84.7 / 97.1 (ModelNet40); the repository is archived ("is now read-only"). — [github.com/salesforce/ULIP](https://github.com/salesforce/ULIP)
- VERIFIED Duoduo CLIP ("a model for 3D representation learning that learns shape encodings from multi-view images instead of point-clouds"): precomputed embeddings for the entire Objaverse dataset from 12 rendered views per object, about 800 MB; "57 A5000 hours and 87 million parameters" against "480 A100 hours to train 1 billion model parameters" for the point-cloud state of the art; text-to-shape retrieval script provided. The README states no licence and GitHub's licence endpoint for the repository answers 404 (no licence file detected). — [github.com/3dlg-hcvc/DuoduoCLIP](https://github.com/3dlg-hcvc/DuoduoCLIP)
- VERIFIED Duoduo CLIP numbers: Objaverse-LVIS top-1 / top-3 / top-5 = 55.2 / 77.1 / 83.4 with 12 views (OpenShape 46.8 / 69.1 / 77.0; Uni3D 55.3 / 76.7 / 82.9; ULIP 26.8 / 44.8 / 52.6); a plain zero-shot CLIP B-32 on the same multi-view renders reaches 35.7 / 54.8 / 62.1; top-1 rises by 11.22 points from 1 to 12 views; on real multi-view photos (MVPNet) OpenShape gets 10.80 top-1 against 59.23 for Duoduo CLIP fine-tuned on MVPNet with one view; image-to-shape retrieval on real images is shown qualitatively only. — [Duoduo CLIP paper (arXiv 2406.11579)](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11579)
- UNVERIFIED (search summaries): the ABO paper's own benchmark (a rendered view of an ABO model as query, the product's catalogue photos as targets; 7,953 meshes, 398,212 catalogue images) reports only 30.0 % Recall@1 for 2022 metric-learning baselines; "DINO Eats CLIP" (CVPR 2026) argues that CLIP lacks fine-grainedness for open-set 3D object retrieval and that naive fusion of DINOv2 and CLIP features underperforms DINOv2 alone; in a CAD image-similarity test (VideoCAD) DINOv2 ranks the right match at median rank 1 against 6 for CLIP. — [ABO paper (arXiv 2110.06199)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06199), [DINO Eats CLIP (CVPR 2026)](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2026/poster/39937), [VideoCAD (arXiv 2505.24838)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24838)

### Inferences
- The design already used by Atelya (detector label, then appearance) is the one the literature converges on: text / category filter first (CLIP is better for broad recall), DINOv2 last (better top-1), on a segmented crop, against 8 or more rendered views per model, then a second pass over more orientations. Keeping several candidates and deciding with size / proportion checks is supported by OSCAR (CLIP's top-10 above 90 % on the small set) and by Diorama (accuracy rises with more retrieved candidates).
- Instance-level precision does not scale: with DINOv2 the top-1 goes from 77 % (21 objects) to 36 % once 1,030 distractors are added, and to 42 % among 194 look-alike household objects. With a furniture catalogue of 8,000 to 25,000 models, "the first neighbour is the very same piece" will be the exception even when the piece is present; "one of the first ten is acceptable" is the realistic use.
- When the object is absent from the catalogue (the normal case for a real apartment), these methods return the nearest look-alike; the only quality measure published is shape distance (Diorama), not "would a person call it the same".
- Multi-view image encoders now equal point-cloud encoders on Objaverse-LVIS (Duoduo CLIP 55.2, Uni3D 55.3, ULIP-2 50.6, OpenShape 46.8) and plain CLIP on renders already gives 35.7: for a catalogue of a few thousand furniture models, thumbnails + a strong image encoder is a sound zero-cost baseline; the 3D-native encoders are an optional upgrade, not a prerequisite.
- Licence position under the project's rule ("the licence of the weights counts"): none of the learned 3D encoders is clean. OpenShape: Apache 2.0 code, weights without licence, trained partly on ShapeNet and 3D-FUTURE (non-commercial). Uni3D: MIT repository, weights without licence tag, trained on the same kind of "ensemble". ULIP-2: BSD code, no weights licence, ShapeNet involved, repository archived. Duoduo CLIP: no licence at all for code or embeddings, so no right of commercial use is granted by default. Only the plain image / text encoders already cleared by the project (DINOv2, Apache 2.0) pass without writing to the authors.
- Compute is not a constraint: views are rendered once in Blender, DINOv2 already runs on the 8 GB card, and the large 3D encoders would fit rented cloud GPUs.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark measures retrieval for REAL furniture photos against a large open furniture catalogue (top-k "same model" or human-judged "very close"). It has to be measured on the project's own logements.
- OSCAR does not state whether relevance means the exact instance or a similar model on Housecat6D; its abstract quotes "an average precision of 90.48%" on YCB-V that does not appear in the ablation table.
- Licences of CLIP / SigLIP weights were not re-opened here (prior knowledge: OpenCLIP code MIT, SigLIP Apache 2.0; UNVERIFIED).
- Precomputed embeddings of Objaverse: Duoduo CLIP's exist (800 MB) but are unlicensed; OpenShape's training-data repository holds image / text features, licence not stated; Uni3D's not checked.

## 3. Adapting the retrieved model to the photo for free: scaling, deformation, retexturing

### Takeaway
Rescaling the retrieved model to the measured width / depth / height and recolouring it is free and already within Atelya's reach; learned "retrieve and deform" methods exist only as research code tied to ShapeNet-type data, and published CAD-retrieval pipelines still place the retrieved model correctly for only about a fifth to a third of objects from a single indoor image (17.6 % reported for ROCA on real ScanNet images, 28-37 % for Diorama on its benchmark scenes).

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED: the Diorama authors list deforming and texturing retrieved CAD models as future work, because retrieval "may introduce mismatches in geometry and appearance". — [Diorama (arXiv 2411.19492)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19492v2)
- VERIFIED: Diorama's 9-degree-of-freedom alignment of the retrieved model is correct for 0.37 of objects with ground-truth depth and 0.28 with estimated depth (Metric3D v2), against 0.27 / 0.24 for GigaPose; one ablation gives 0.11 with a single retrieved candidate against 0.23 with eight. — [Diorama (arXiv 2411.19492)](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19492v2)
- VERIFIED licences: ROCA's repository has a LICENSE file whose text was not shown on the page, and "Our project depends on ShapeNet, ScanNet, and Scan2CAD datasets"; DiffCAD's repository is listed as "CC0-1.0 license", "achives robuts cross-domain performance while only trained on synthetic dataset", and names 3D-FRONT / 3D-FUTURE, ShapeNet and ScanNet25k as data. — [github.com/cangumeli/ROCA](https://github.com/cangumeli/ROCA), [github.com/daoyig/DiffCAD](https://github.com/daoyig/DiffCAD)
- UNVERIFIED (search summaries of abstracts): ROCA raised retrieval-aware alignment accuracy on ScanNet images "from 9.5% to 17.6%"; FastCAD improves Scan2CAD alignment accuracy in the video setting "from 43.0% to 48.2%" and reconstruction accuracy from 22.9 % to 29.6 %; DiffCAD is reported to "surpass the supervised state-of-the-art on the Scan2CAD dataset by 5.9% with 8 hypotheses"; "Zero-shot Inexact CAD Model Alignment from a Single Image" (ICCV 2025) builds on DINOv2 features and reports +12.7 % over the supervised SPARC on 20 unseen categories. — [ROCA (arXiv 2112.01988)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.01988), [DiffCAD (arXiv 2311.18610)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18610), [Zero-shot inexact CAD alignment (arXiv 2507.03292)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03292)
- UNVERIFIED (search summaries): "Joint Learning of 3D Shape Retrieval and Deformation" (Uy et al., CVPR 2021, Adobe Research) trains a deformation module together with the retrieval embedding so that retrieval favours shapes that deform well to the target; KP-RED (CVPR 2024) uses semantic keypoints and a neural-cage deformation and "surpasses existing state-of-the-art approaches by a large margin on PartNet and Scan2CAD". Code licences were not found. — [Adobe Research publication page](https://research.adobe.com/publication/joint-learning-of-3d-shape-retrieval-and-deformation), [KP-RED (CVPR 2024)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/html/Zhang_KP-RED_Exploiting_Semantic_Keypoints_for_Joint_3D_Shape_Retrieval_and_CVPR_2024_paper.html)

### Inferences
- The trained CAD-retrieval and retrieve-and-deform systems (ROCA, DiffCAD, FastCAD, SPARC, Uy et al., KP-RED) are trained and evaluated on ShapeNet, PartNet, ScanNet and 3D-FUTURE, all non-commercial: even where the code is permissive (DiffCAD: CC0), the weights and the CAD pool are not usable in the product. What transfers is the method, re-implemented on a legal catalogue with DINOv2 features, which is the direction of the 2025 zero-shot papers.
- Non-uniform scaling to the measured bounding box is the adaptation with the best value: free, deterministic, and it fixes the most visible error (size). It distorts details (legs, handles and cushions get thicker or thinner), so it should be bounded (for example ±20-25 % per axis, to be tuned) and combined with choosing the candidate whose proportions already match best.
- Colour / material transfer from the photo (dominant colour per part, or swapping for a catalogue material of the same family) is feasible with bricks the project already has; projecting the photo itself as a texture only covers the visible side and bakes in the photo's lighting.
- Atelya's situation is easier than these benchmarks on one point: the room geometry and the camera positions are known, so the alignment problem (where and how big) is largely solved by its own geometry; the open problem is the identity of the piece.

### Gaps
- No source found that compares "retrieve + deform" against image-to-3D generation specifically on furniture fidelity.
- No permissively licensed, ready-to-use part-aware deformation tool for furniture was identified; licences of Uy et al. and KP-RED code not opened.
- Photo-to-texture transfer methods and their licences were not researched in this pass.

## 4. Procedural generators: which open tools can BUILD furniture, kitchens and bathroom fixtures from parameters?

### Takeaway
Infinigen Indoors (Princeton, BSD 3-Clause, built on Blender 4.2) is the only large open procedural library: 79 generators in the paper and 89 entries in today's repository, including sofas, chairs, beds, tables, shelves, kitchen cabinets and islands, sinks, bathtubs, toilets, ovens, dishwashers, fridges, TVs and lamps, each exportable alone to OBJ / FBX / USD with baked textures; it yields plausible generic furniture driven by parameters (well suited to fitted kitchens, bathrooms and storage) rather than a copy of a given piece; the Blender add-ons that generate cabinetry are GPL tools whose output is ours, but the free ones are old or minimally maintained.

### Inferences
- Infinigen: the BSD licence covers the generator code; the meshes and baked textures it produces come from procedural code (some of it CC0), not from third-party assets, so they can ship in the Unreal product. Only if the generator code itself were redistributed would the BSD notice have to follow.
- Integration: Infinigen is tied to Blender 4.2 (bpy 4.2 as a Python module, or a 4.2 Blender) and Python 3.11, while Atelya uses Blender 5.2. It should therefore run as a SEPARATE tool that writes .blend / USD / OBJ / FBX files which Atelya's Blender 5.2 then imports. Native Windows is only "experimental" for the minimal install; rented Linux cloud machines are the supported case. Installing it requires the owner's written agreement (project rule).
- Parameter control is the real work: the paper says parameters can be overridden and factories are Python classes, but the user documentation only exposes the factory name and a count. Driving a generator to given dimensions and style (for example a 3.2 m run of base cabinets with five doors and an island) means writing Python against each factory and checking that the result respects the requested size.
- Exported materials are baked images of procedural shaders (albedo, roughness, normal, metal); glass and other transparent parts need manual handling in Unreal.
- GPL add-ons: the GPL binds the add-on's code; geometry produced by running it is the user's work (GNU FAQ), and on the official Blender platform any asset shipped inside an add-on must be CC0. The caution remains for add-ons obtained elsewhere that bundle ready-made meshes or textures under GPL or CC BY-SA: those files are content, not output.
- Among free Blender add-ons, none is a maintained, ready kitchen / furniture generator for Blender 5.x: Archimesh is "as is", Home Builder's open repository is an unfinished migration, free Archipack targets Blender 2.79. They are sources of ideas (parametric cabinet logic) more than drop-in bricks; the same logic can be written directly in Atelya's own geometry code, which already builds plinths, placard doors and cornices.
- Best fit for procedural generation: fitted and repetitive items that catalogues cover badly and that must match the room exactly (kitchen cabinet runs, islands, worktops, wardrobes, shelving, sinks, bathtubs, toilets, doors, windows, stairs). Worst fit: reproducing one specific designer chair or sofa.

### Gaps
- blender.org/about/license returned HTTP 403; Blender's own sentence on ownership of output was not opened (the GNU FAQ and the Blender manual cover the point).
- Visual realism of Infinigen single assets next to real photos: no independent evaluation found (the paper's human study rates whole scenes).
- Sverchok (node-based parametric geometry, believed GPL v3) was not opened.
- Whether Infinigen runs under Blender 5.x was not found; the changelog only states 4.2.0.

## 5. Overall: how close can "pick or generate the closest model, then adapt it" get to "the same furniture as in the photo" at zero cost?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports "right category, right size, similar style and colour" for most common furniture, and "the very same piece" only when the exact product happens to be in the catalogue and is ranked first, which measured retrieval does less than half the time once the catalogue exceeds a thousand items; no study measures a "very close" share for real apartment photos, so that share must be measured on the project's own logements.

### Cited Findings
- VERIFIED: when the exact model is in a small library, appearance-based retrieval finds it (ACDC: category and model preserved for 39 of 39 objects in four simulated scenes; OSCAR: 77.38 % top-1 among 21 objects; CMIC: 74.9 % top-1 for seen objects on Pix3D). — [ACDC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07408v2), [OSCAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07333v1), [arXiv 2401.00405](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00405v1)
- VERIFIED: the rate drops quickly with catalogue size and look-alikes: 36.41 % (DINOv2) to 41.31 % (CLIP) top-1 after adding 1,030 distractor models; 41.81 % among 194 household objects of 10 categories. — [OSCAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07333v1)
- VERIFIED: for objects never seen in training, supervised retrieval of the right shape among a few hundred to a few thousand candidates gives 0.3 % top-1 without special training and 24-34 % top-1 (about 62 % top-5) with it on Pix3D; 50-59 % on the synthetic MOOS set. — [arXiv 2401.00405](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00405v1)
- VERIFIED: the authors of retrieval-based scene systems present the result as approximate: "applications requiring precise reconstructions may be hindered by the lack of exact geometry and texture matches." (Diorama); "ACDC is bounded by the diversity of its underlying asset dataset", with over 10,000 assets judged "insufficient to densely capture the real-world distribution of objects." — [Diorama](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19492v2), [ACDC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07408v2)
- VERIFIED scale of the free legal pool: about 19,000 furniture-tagged Objaverse objects before licence and quality filtering, 85 Poly Haven furniture models, over 22,000 free Blendkit models of all kinds, 507 + 202 + 82 NVIDIA pack assets, 89 Infinigen indoor generators, plus ABO already in use. — sources in sections 1 and 4.

### Inferences
- Order of magnitude, given as a judgement and not as a measured fact: with ABO + filtered Objaverse CC BY / CC0 + Poly Haven (+ Blendkit and NVIDIA packs if their terms are accepted), a category filter, DINOv2 on rendered views, bounded rescaling and recolouring, a look-alike that a client accepts as plausible should be found for most mainstream pieces (sofas, beds, dining tables, chairs, shelves, lamps). A piece a client would call "the same" will be the exception: mass-market designs that someone happened to model and publish under CC BY / CC0, and simple boxy furniture where proportions and colour are almost everything (wardrobes, sideboards, bookcases, kitchen runs), for which procedural generation can be exact in size.
- Enlarging the catalogue helps coverage but hurts ranking (more look-alikes): past a few thousand models per category, gains depend on re-ranking with extra cues the project already has (measured size and proportions, colour, the sort given by the photo reading), not on the encoder alone.
- What remains impossible at zero cost with this route: a guaranteed exact twin of an arbitrary piece; distinctive shapes (designer chairs, carved or antique furniture, unusual lamps); fabric patterns, wood grain and wear identical to the photo; the unseen sides of the piece; one-off built-ins unless a generator is written for them; and legal certainty about third-party design rights on Sketchfab-origin models.
- The realistic target of this route is therefore "same family, same size, same colour, compatible style", plus exact-size procedural solutions for fitted elements; "the same as in the photo" needs either the manufacturer's own model or a per-object reconstruction / generation from the photos (both covered by other researchers).

### Gaps
- No published measurement of the share of real household furniture for which a free catalogue contains a "very close" model, nor of human-judged similarity after retrieve-and-adapt. The project's fidelity bench (photos of app-001, app-002, app-004) is the place to measure it.
